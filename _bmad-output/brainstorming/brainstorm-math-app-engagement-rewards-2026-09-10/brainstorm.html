Technique 2

Cross-Pollination

Five unrelated worlds were tuned in like radio stations, each broadcasting a lesson borrowed for RekenRaket.

🎰

Casino industry

  • Variable-ratio reward schedule — occasional bigger rewards at unpredictable intervals, for genuine surprise-and-delight rather than compulsion.
  • Reframe a wrong answer as a "near miss" — show how close the number was (so close, 7 vs 8).
  • A "jackpot moment" — finishing a full Leitner box triggers a big celebratory animation.
  • Flip the lesson: frame streaks as a growing plant to nurture, not a losable asset to protect.
🎮

Video games

  • Collectible creatures unlocked by mastering specific fact families.
  • An XP bar that fills per session, capped so no session feels like "not enough".
  • A "boss-level" review day with fun boss-battle graphics for the hardest cards.
  • Earn a hint token after a missed day — a friendly clue, not a penalty.
  • A world map where each new topic unlocks a new island.
  • Avatar/costume customization unlocked by streaks, decoupled from correctness.
🏅

Sports coaching

  • Praise effort and strategy out loud — "you tried three ways!"
  • A post-session recap highlighting one specific improvement versus yesterday.
  • A warm-up round of easy problems to build momentum.
  • A cool-down round that always ends on an easy, confidence-building question.
🐾

Pet-raising games

  • A virtual pet fed by completed exercises, whose mood reflects consistency, not perfection.
  • The pet reacts warmly even after a rough day — it still wants to try again tomorrow.
🧠

Child psychology

  • Replace "wrong" with "not yet" language throughout the UI.
  • An emotion check-in (tap a face) at session start and end, feeding the adaptive tone system.
  • Let him choose which exercise type to start with, for a sense of control.
  • A predictable daily ritual — same intro animation each time.
  • Calming background music during sessions the system detects as high-mistake.
  • A friendly narrator character who speaks instructions aloud instead of relying on text.

Technique 5

SCAMPER

Applied to the emerging favorite concept: a growing rocket companion tied to consistent practice, with Calm Day Mode for rough days.

S

Substitute

  • Swap the rocket theme for a growing garden, in case space isn't his thing — same growth mechanic.
  • Swap point-based rewards for a customizable avatar wardrobe.
  • Replace numeric feedback like 80% with descriptive milestone badges (e.g. Addition Explorer).
C

Combine

  • Merge the Leitner box concept visually with the rocket's fuel tanks — each box fills as cards graduate.
  • Merge the daily mood check-in with the Calm Day Mode trigger and a parent-visible wellbeing note.
  • Merge the parent view with the child's story — a short narrative recap instead of a bare stats dashboard.
A

Adapt

  • Adapt spaced-repetition flashcard UI from language-learning apps — slower pacing, simpler text.
  • Adapt the physical trading-card sticker-album pattern into a digital collectible album.
  • Adapt boss-battle framing into a big-test day, appearing only after many successes, never on a bad day.
M

Modify (magnify / minify)

  • Magnify: milestone celebrations (finishing a whole topic) become big and cinematic.
  • Minify: shrink the failure state entirely — no failure screen, only "let's try that one again later."
  • Magnify: the very first session's onboarding becomes a big memorable launch-day moment.
  • Modify: shrink difficulty granularity into very small steps, so mistake-heavy days read as normal variance.
P

Put to other use

  • Repurpose the mastered-cards list as a trophy shelf screen parents and grandparents can browse.
  • Turn practice data into a simple weekly math-adventure story recap parents can read aloud together.
E

Eliminate

  • Remove timers and countdowns entirely for a 7-year-old — no pressure cues on hard days.
  • Remove any leaderboard or comparison-to-others feature outright.
R

Reverse

  • Instead of the app pushing him to practice, let him initiate by feeding or checking on his companion.
  • Instead of always ending when he is done, let the app sometimes suggest stopping — modeling healthy limits.
  • Instead of hardest cards last, occasionally front-load one easy win first for immediate momentum.

Audiobook apps

Mission synthesis

Everything converges on one rocket

Six techniques kept surfacing the same three ideas from different angles — here is where they collide.

🚀
Convergent theme 1 Reward decoupled from correctness, tied to consistency — never-subtract points, grace token, Streak Saver, Duolingo streak-freeze are one mechanic, not four.
Convergent theme 2 Never punish — no red X / buzzer, no leaderboard, no timers, no failure screen, no streak reset. A hard design constraint, not a feature choice.
Convergent theme 3 Feedback moves from binary right/wrong to a continuous warm signal — near-miss framing, "not yet" language, and "wiggle don't redden" are the same redesign in three costumes.
💥Breakthrough connection

The rocket-fuel-tank visualization of Leitner boxes (from SCAMPER) solves the reading-beginner abstraction problem directly — a 7yo can see fuel filling up without reading "box 3 of 5". This single visual metaphor should be prioritized early, since it also carries the reward and progress systems.

💥Breakthrough connection

Calm Day Mode is not a separate feature but an orchestration layer — it activates existing pieces (confidence cards, gentler audio tone, shorter session, guaranteed small reward) based on a rising mistake-rate signal, so building it mainly requires a trigger plus a bundle of already-designed responses.

🚀 Final launch checklist — chosen directions

  1. Companion / rocket-growth metaphor, mapping visually to the Leitner boxes.
  2. Audio-first, icon-first UI, eliminating reliance on reading.
  3. Grace mechanic that protects streaks without ever punishing.
  4. Auto-triggered Calm Day Mode on high-mistake days.
  5. Parent-facing story / trophy recap bridging app rewards to real-world praise.
